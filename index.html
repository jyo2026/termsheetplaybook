The problem

Founders are using ChatGPT to understand
term sheets and getting the wrong answers.

Generic AI tools do not cite Indian law. Your founders deserve better before they walk into your office.

Generic AI tools get Indian law wrong

ChatGPT and Claude sometimes reference UK or US law sources, cite outdated positions, or hallucinate market practice. For a founder reading about their term sheet, that is a real risk.

Founders sign what they don't understand

Early-stage founders rely on AI-generated summaries of their term sheets and get answers grounded in foreign law or generic common law, not the Companies Act 2013 or FEMA.

No India-specific playbook exists

Existing resources were written for global markets. They don't cover what Indian legislation actually constrains, what is genuinely negotiable, or what market standard looks like here.

How it works

Three steps from question to answer.

A straightforward RAG pipeline built for Indian deal rooms — no hallucination, no foreign precedent.

1
Ask a question about a clause in the term sheet.

Type your question in plain English — about liquidation preference, anti-dilution, drag-along, or any other provision you're working through.

2
The RAG engine retrieves the relevant legal material.

The system searches an India-specific knowledge base: Companies Act 2013, FEMA, and the NDI Rules 2019, rather than sourcing from unverified sources.

3
The answer explains both perspectives, with citations.

You get a structured response covering the founder counsel and investor counsel positions, grounded in the legislation that actually governs your deal.

What it does

Better preparation before the kickoff call.

Built for founders who need structured legal reasoning — fast, grounded in Indian law, and honest about its limits.

Both sides of the table

Every clause is analysed from founder counsel and investor counsel perspectives. Understand what the other side actually wants before you pick up the phone.

Grounded in Indian law

Companies Act 2013, FEMA, and the NDI Rules 2019. Not US precedents. Not generic common law. The statutes and market practice that actually govern your deal.

Query in plain English

"Is put option enforceable in India?" "What's market on anti-dilution for seed?" Get structured answers with legislation cited — not guesses.

Red flags surfaced

Know which clauses are negotiating traps, which are standard, and which depend entirely on whether you're acting for the founder or the fund.

Honest about its limits

If a query falls outside the knowledge base, the tool clearly says so. It acknowledges its limits rather than speculating.
